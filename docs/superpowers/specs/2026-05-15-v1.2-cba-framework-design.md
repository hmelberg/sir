# v1.2 — Two-layer cost-benefit framework with discounting, substitution, and explicit cost streams

**Date:** 2026-05-15
**Status:** Draft for review
**Builds on:** v1 (`2026-05-14-sir-microfoundations-abm-design.md`), v1.1 (`2026-05-14-v1.1-healthcare-and-comparison-design.md`)

## 1. Purpose

The current welfare ledger bundles too much into single scalars (`V_a`, `c_vax`, `α_workplace`), making policy comparison hard to defend. This spec introduces a two-layer architecture:

- **Behavioral layer** (unchanged from v1.1): agent FOCs use bundled scalars to drive choice. These are *taste parameters*, not directly comparable to monetary data.
- **Social CBA layer** (new): a separate post-processor decomposes outcomes into measurable cost streams (medical, productivity, mortality, behavioral, programmatic). Each stream has its own calibration source and its own PSA uncertainty.

The split lets the model produce policy reports in standard CBA form — tidy tables of per-stream costs and benefits with units, monetary equivalents where possible, and explicit handling of discounting, behavioral substitution, and voluntary-vs-forced reduction.

This is what the package needs to be useful for actual policy advice (within the limits of the disclaimer).

## 2. Scope

### In scope (v1.2)

- New `sir.cba` module: pure post-processor producing a `CBAReport` from a `SimResult` (+ optional `HealthcareOutcomes`).
- Explicit decomposition of social costs into ~9 streams (see §5).
- Discount rate applied to time-distributed costs and benefits.
- Behavioral substitution in the ABM: a configurable fraction of lost group attendance redistributes to other groups.
- Voluntary-vs-forced reduction made explicit: `binding_fraction` reported per intervention.
- Comparison wrapper extended: `run_comparison(..., cba=CBAConfig)` adds CBA reports to baseline and treatment, with per-stream delta in `outcome_deltas()`.
- Calibration defaults aligned with COVID-era literature; documented sources.

### Out of scope (v1.2; potential v1.3+)

- **Equity weighting**: aggregate welfare summed across agents. Distributional weighting (per-age, per-income) deferred.
- **Capacity-dependent mortality**: hospitals can over-capacity raise the death rate. Still a static rate per age.
- **Endogenous vaccination choice**: vaccination still policy-controlled.
- **Heterogeneous vaccine efficacy**: `v_eff` remains a population-average scalar.
- **Per-agent income heterogeneity**: production-loss costs use a single `wage_per_day_by_age` rather than agent-specific incomes.
- **Time-varying parameters**: medical costs, wages, etc. are constant within a run.
- **Multi-currency support**: all monetary streams in one (configurable) currency unit.

### Why two layers

The behavioral layer cares about what agents perceive. An agent doesn't reason about "VSL × YLL × IFR" — they have a felt cost of getting sick. That belongs to `V_a`.

The social layer cares about what's policy-relevant and *measurable*. Decision-makers don't optimize a representative-agent utility; they trade off lives, dollars, productivity, education. Those need separate accounting with separate calibration sources.

Trying to do both in one ledger conflates them and forces V_a to do too many things at once.

## 4. Cost-benefit streams

The CBA report tracks **nine independent streams**, each in well-defined units:

| # | Stream | Unit | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Direct medical costs** | $ | `hospital_cost_per_day · Σ hosp_prev + icu_cost_per_day · Σ icu_prev + outpatient_cost_per_case · new_inf` |
| 2 | **Vaccination program costs** | $ | `dose_cost · new_vaccinees + admin_cost · new_vaccinees` |
| 3 | **Lost productivity from illness** | $ | `wage_per_day · sick_days_per_inf · new_infections` (age-weighted) |
| 4 | **Lost productivity from death** | $ | `present_value(annual_productivity · remaining_working_years · deaths)` (age-weighted) |
| 5 | **Years of life lost (mortality)** | YLL | `Σ_a yll_per_death_a · deaths_a` |
| 6 | **Acute morbidity** | QALYs | `disability_weight · sick_days / 365 · new_inf` |
| 7 | **Behavioral utility loss** | $ (via VOT) | `value_of_time · Δ(attendance_hours)` |
| 8 | **Precaution effort cost** | $ (via WTP) | `precaution_effort_cost_per_unit · Σ e` |
| 9 | **Direct + spillover policy costs** | $ | from intervention `direct_cost_per_day` and `spillover_cost_fn` |

Each stream is computed independently from simulation outputs. No cross-stream subtraction or sharing — they're additive, with no risk of double-counting.

### Monetization conventions

Streams in $ are directly summable. Streams in YLL or QALYs are reported separately and can optionally be monetized via `value_per_qaly` and `value_per_yll` parameters. This separation lets reports show:

- **Total cost** in money (streams 1-4 + 7-9)
- **Health impact** in YLL/QALY (streams 5-6)
- **Cost-effectiveness ratio**: `Δ$ / ΔQALY` and `Δ$ / ΔYLL`

The conventional decision rule (do the intervention if `$/QALY < threshold`) becomes a single derived number from the report.

### Avoiding double-counting

Three relationships to be careful about:

- **`V_a` vs. mortality**: `V_a` drives behavior but is NOT in the CBA report. The CBA report computes mortality directly from deaths × VSL (stream 4) or deaths × YLL (stream 5). No overlap.
- **`c_vax` vs. vaccination costs**: same logic. `c_vax` is the agent's perceived cost; the CBA report uses `dose_cost + admin_cost + side_effect_disutility` from vaccinees.
- **Attendance utility vs. productivity**: This is the trickiest one. The agent's `α_workplace · log(1+m_work)` bundles both intrinsic work value AND wage. The CBA separates them: stream 3 captures productivity (in $), and stream 7 captures only the *intrinsic + behavioral* portion of attendance loss. To prevent double-counting, the conversion from attendance loss to $ uses a `value_of_time` rate that explicitly excludes wages (which are in stream 3 separately).

Documentation in the spec will make these boundaries explicit. PSA on the conversion parameters lets users probe sensitivity.

## 5. `CBAConfig`

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class CBAConfig:
    # Discount rate (annual) applied to time-distributed costs
    discount_rate: float = 0.03

    # Direct medical costs (per day or per case)
    hospital_cost_per_day: float = 1500.0       # USD-ish
    icu_cost_per_day: float = 4000.0
    outpatient_cost_per_case: float = 200.0

    # Vaccination program costs (per dose)
    vax_dose_cost: float = 25.0
    vax_admin_cost: float = 30.0
    vax_sideeffect_value: float = 50.0          # WTP-style disutility per recipient

    # Productivity from illness (per day per case, age-weighted)
    wage_per_day_by_age: tuple[float, ...] = (
        0.0, 0.0, 200.0, 250.0, 280.0, 260.0, 50.0,
    )  # kids/elderly low/zero; working-age peak
    sick_days_per_infection: float = 7.0       # mean lost days per non-hospitalized case
    sick_days_hospitalized: float = 14.0        # additional days for hospitalized
    sick_days_icu: float = 21.0

    # Mortality monetization
    value_per_yll: float = 100_000.0            # value of a life-year (VSL/life_expectancy)
    remaining_working_years_by_age: tuple[float, ...] = (
        45.0, 40.0, 35.0, 25.0, 15.0, 5.0, 0.0,
    )

    # Acute morbidity (QALY)
    disability_weight_symptomatic: float = 0.15
    disability_weight_hospitalized: float = 0.50
    disability_weight_icu: float = 0.80

    # Behavioral monetization
    value_of_time_per_hour: float = 25.0        # excluding wage (already in stream 3)

    # Whether to monetize health outcomes (otherwise report separately)
    monetize_health: bool = False
```

## 6. `CBAReport`

```python
@dataclass
class CBAReport:
    # Per-stream totals (sum over time, discounted)
    streams: dict[str, float]  # {'direct_medical': X, 'productivity_illness': Y, ...}

    total_monetary_cost: float      # streams 1-4 + 7-9
    total_health_burden_yll: float  # stream 5
    total_health_burden_qaly: float # stream 6

    # If monetize_health=True, also includes monetized health
    total_cost_including_health: float | None

    # Time series for plotting
    per_day: dict[str, np.ndarray]  # discounted contribution per day

    def summary(self) -> pd.DataFrame:
        """Tidy table: stream | unit | value | as_monetary (if applicable)."""

    def cost_effectiveness_vs(self, baseline: 'CBAReport') -> dict:
        """Return $/YLL averted, $/QALY gained, etc."""
```

## 7. Discounting

Costs occurring on day $t$ are discounted by $(1 + r)^{-t/365}$ where $r$ is the annual rate. With $r = 0.03$:

- A cost on day 365 is discounted by $\approx 0.97$
- A cost on day 1825 (5 years) discounted by $\approx 0.86$

For deaths, the YLL stream applies discounting *within* the YLL — i.e., a death at day 100 with YLL=20 contributes:

$$\text{discounted YLL} = \int_0^{20} e^{-rt} dt = \frac{1 - e^{-20r}}{r}$$

which for $r=0.03$ ≈ 15.0 (a 25% discount on the nominal 20 years). This is the standard convention for QALY/YLL discounting.

Discount-rate = 0 should be allowed for users who prefer undiscounted accounting.

## 8. Behavioral substitution

When an intervention removes attendance from one group (e.g., school closure), the model currently assumes those contact-hours simply vanish. In reality, lost contacts partially substitute to other groups (kids stay home with parents, get together in the park, etc.).

### Proposed mechanism

Each `Intervention` gains an optional `substitution: dict[GroupType, float]` parameter mapping destination groups to substitution fractions. When attendance is reduced in the targeted group by amount $\Delta m$, attendance increases in destination groups proportionally. Total contact hours stay constant if fractions sum to 1; less if fractions sum to less.

Defaults for common interventions (calibration-grade, not data-grade):

| Intervention | Substitution map |
|---|---|
| `close_schools` | `{HOUSEHOLD: 0.7, COMMUNITY: 0.2}` (sum 0.9: 10% net reduction) |
| `wfh_mandate` | `{HOUSEHOLD: 0.8, COMMUNITY: 0.1}` (sum 0.9) |
| `gathering_limit` | `{HOUSEHOLD: 0.4, COMMUNITY: 0.2}` (sum 0.6: 40% net reduction) |
| `event_ban` | `{COMMUNITY: 0.3}` (sum 0.3: 70% net reduction) |

The CBA report flags substitution because it affects the *effective* m reduction (intervention strength) and the *welfare cost* (lost attendance partially replaced).

### Voluntary-vs-forced and substitution

These compose: an intervention that targets 1000 attendees has:

- **Effective binding**: of those 1000, how many would have attended anyway (intervention has 'bite')?
- **Substitution destination**: where do the bound contacts go?

The CBA report computes both:

```python
report.binding_fraction = {intervention_name: 0.65, ...}
report.substitution_breakdown = {intervention_name: {GroupType.HOUSEHOLD: 0.5, ...}, ...}
```

This makes the policy-attribution explicit. Without these, "averted X deaths from school closure" is unattributable.

The `outcome_deltas()` table gets additional rows for each CBA stream, with units shown in the table.

The `cba_summary()` method gives a more focused view: just the CBA streams, sorted by absolute delta, with cost-effectiveness ratios computed automatically.

## 11. Verification strategy

| Test | Expected |
|---|---|
| Zero infections | All medical/productivity/mortality streams zero |
| Zero discount rate | Discounted totals match undiscounted sums |
| `monetize_health=False` | `total_cost_including_health` is None |
| `monetize_health=True` | Equals `total_monetary_cost + total_yll × value_per_yll` |
| Comparison with cba | `cba_summary` has all 9 streams |
| Substitution sum = 0 | No substitution; matches v1.1 behavior |
| Substitution sum = 1 | Total contact hours unchanged (just relocated) |
| Cost-effectiveness ratio | Sensible sign and magnitude for known scenarios |

## 12. Open design questions

1. **VSL or YLL as the primary mortality metric?** Two conventions exist. VSL is more common in CBA practice (~$10M/death); YLL is more common in health economics (~$100k/year × remaining life expectancy). The spec proposes YLL as primary with an optional VSL alternative. **Recommendation: keep YLL primary; offer VSL as a derived measure** (`vsl = sum(yll_per_death · value_per_yll)` per death).

2. **Substitution fractions as user input vs. calibrated defaults?** The defaults above are guesses; real values come from time-use studies. **Recommendation: ship defaults and document the sources; expose via `Intervention` constructor.**

3. **Should we deprecate `V_a` as a single bundled parameter?** Argument for: forces clarity. Argument against: `V_a` is what drives the FOC, which is structural. **Recommendation: keep `V_a` as a behavioral parameter, document its non-overlap with CBA streams in the docs.**

4. **Discounting per day vs. annual compounding?** Both are conventional. Annual is simpler to read; daily is more accurate. **Recommendation: daily, matching simulation time-step.**

5. **Should `CBAReport` include a single "social welfare" number?** The user might want one bottom line. **Recommendation: yes, computed as `total_monetary_cost + monetized_health` if `monetize_health=True`, otherwise leave the two parts separate and report cost-effectiveness ratios.**

## 13. Success criteria

The CBA framework succeeds if a user can answer the following from a single `run_comparison()` call:

- *"What is the monetary cost of a school closure vs. baseline?"* — Difference in `total_monetary_cost`
- *"What is the cost-effectiveness ratio of vaccination?"* — `$/YLL averted` from `cost_effectiveness_vs`
- *"Of the total cost of intervention X, how much is medical vs. productivity loss vs. behavioral?"* — Stream-by-stream breakdown
- *"How does the answer change if we believe productivity loss is 20% higher than baseline?"* — PSA on `wage_per_day_by_age`
- *"What fraction of school closure's restriction is binding (vs. voluntary reduction)?"* — `binding_fraction[close_schools]`

Each is a single number or table from the report.

- Phases 1-4 + 7 + 8 (just the CBA streams): ~900 LoC
- Doesn't touch the dynamics; pure post-processing
- Delivers the policy table

**Track B (Substitution): model refinement, independent benefit**
- Phases 5-6 + tests: ~500 LoC
- Touches the ABM
- Better intervention realism

If shipped separately, the spec can be split into v1.2 (Track A, CBA) and v1.3 (Track B, substitution). Recommendation: **do Track A first** since it delivers the user's primary ask. Substitution can follow.
