Microfounded SIR — interactive model

An epidemic model where behavior is a choice

Reframing SIR so that disease spread depends on how often people meet, how much care they take, and the costs and benefits of doing so.

The classical SIR model lumps everything about how a disease spreads into a single parameter, $\beta$. This makes it hard to reason about real-world interventions. School closures, mask mandates, and behavioral change all act on different parts of $\beta$ — but if $\beta$ is a single number, those distinctions disappear.

This model decomposes $\beta$ into two interpretable components:

$$\beta = m \cdot p$$

where $m$ is the number of meetings per person per day and $p$ is the probability of transmission per meeting.

Interventions act on the components, not on $\beta$ directly. Cancelling soccer practice reduces $m$ for those who play. Mandating masks reduces $p$ everywhere people gather. Vaccination removes susceptibles. Each policy becomes a concrete adjustment to a specific parameter — and the model produces a welfare comparison across policies.

People also adjust on their own. When more people are sick, rational individuals reduce their meetings and take more precautions. This is built into the model as an optimization: each agent chooses contacts $\theta \in [0,1]$ and precaution effort $e \in [0,1]$ to maximize their flow utility net of expected infection cost.

  • Meeting rate, transmission probability, recovery rate
  • Cost of getting infected and of taking precaution

Detailed model

Full mathematical specification of the microfounded SIR model.

1. Disease dynamics

The population of size $N$ is partitioned into susceptible $S$, infected $I$, and recovered $R$. Discrete time steps of one day. The classical mass-action form is:

$$\Delta S_t = -\beta\, \frac{S_t I_t}{N}, \qquad \Delta I_t = \beta\, \frac{S_t I_t}{N} - \gamma I_t, \qquad \Delta R_t = \gamma I_t$$

where $\gamma$ is the recovery rate (so $1/\gamma$ is the mean infectious period).

The reformulation replaces $\beta$ with a contact-based microfoundation. For a susceptible agent, the daily infection probability is:

$$\pi_t = 1 - (1 - p)^{\,m \cdot I_t / N}$$

derived as follows: the agent has $m$ meetings; each is with an infected person with probability $I_t/N$; each such meeting transmits with probability $p$. So the probability of escaping infection across the expected $m \cdot I_t/N$ risky meetings is $(1-p)^{m\cdot I_t/N}$.

At low prevalence this reduces to $\pi_t \approx m p \cdot I_t/N$, which recovers mass-action with $\beta = mp$. The basic reproduction number is:

$$\boxed{R_0 = \frac{m \cdot p}{\gamma}}$$

2. Microfounded behavioral response

Each agent chooses a voluntary attendance multiplier $\theta \in [0, 1]$ (scaling baseline meetings $m_0$ to $\theta m_0$) and a precaution effort $e \in [0, 1]$ (reducing per-meeting transmission to $p(1-e)$). They maximize per-day flow utility:

$$U(\theta, e \mid I/N) = \underbrace{\alpha \log(1 + \theta m_0)}_{\text{utility from meetings}} - \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{2} \kappa\, e^2}_{\text{cost of precaution}} - \underbrace{V \cdot \pi(\theta, e; I/N)}_{\text{expected infection cost}}$$

where:

  • $\alpha$ — utility weight on social attendance
  • $\kappa$ — quadratic cost of precaution effort
  • $V$ — total expected cost of being infected (suffering, mortality risk × VSL, sequelae)
  • $\pi$ — the per-period infection probability (linearized: $p(1-e) \cdot \theta m_0 \cdot I/N$)

The agent's first-order conditions yield interior solutions:

$$\frac{\alpha m_0}{1 + \theta m_0} = V (1 - e) \cdot m_0 p \cdot \frac{I}{N}, \qquad e = \frac{V \cdot \theta m_0 p \cdot I/N}{\kappa}$$

Solved numerically. Rational expectations: agents observe true $I/N$, without lag or media bias. Atomism: each agent takes $I/N$ as exogenous — the gap between private and social optimum is the externality that justifies policy intervention.

3. Interventions

Four classes of intervention map onto the model's components:

ClassActs onExample
Avoidance (mandatory)$m_0 \to f \cdot m_0$School closure, gathering limits
Precaution (mandatory)$p \to f \cdot p$Mask mandate, ventilation rules
Remove infectious$I \to (1-q) \cdot I$Testing and isolation
Remove susceptibles$S \to S - \dot{V}_t$Vaccination

Policy changes shift the constraint set; rational agents re-optimize given the new constraints. Risk compensation emerges naturally: if a mask mandate lowers $p$, optimal $\theta^*$ rises slightly (people feel safer, gather more) — a comparative-statics result, not an extra assumption.

4. Welfare accounting

Aggregate welfare under intervention scenario $\xi$:

$$W(\xi) = \sum_{t=0}^{T} \sum_{i=1}^{N} U_{i,t}(\xi) \;-\; \sum_t \text{policy cost}(\xi, t)$$

The cost-benefit of intervention $\xi$ vs. baseline is simply $\Delta W = W(\xi) - W(0)$. The total decomposes into six interpretable components:

Attendance utility$\alpha \log(1 + \theta m_0)$ summed+

5. Contact structure (full model)

The simulation in this page uses a single composite group with mean-field mixing for tractability. The full ABM in the underlying Python package extends this with a bipartite agent–group structure across seven group types:

TypeStructureSource of structure
HouseholdSmall (1–6), fully connected, dailyCensus composition
Kin (linked households)Cross-household low-frequency mixingSurvey data on extended family contact
School~300–1000 members, age-groupedEnrollment data
Workplace~10–500, well-mixed withinFirm-size distribution
Recurring leisure~10–50 (gyms, choirs, clubs)Time-use surveys
One-off eventsEphemeral (weddings, concerts)Event scheduling
CommunityAge × neighborhood random mixingPOLYMOD contact matrices

This decomposition lets interventions target specific layers: close schools shuts off transmission in just the school layer; WFH mandate scales workplace attendance; bubble rules restrict kin layer; etc.

6. Probabilistic sensitivity analysis

The full model supports PSA: each uncertain parameter is given a distribution (normal, lognormal, beta, triangular, or a 95% CI shorthand), drawn independently per outer iteration, with $n$ stochastic Monte Carlo runs per draw. Results are reported as medians and 95% confidence intervals on outcomes (welfare, peak infected, cumulative attack rate by age group), and as tornado plots of per-parameter rank correlation with each outcome.

7. Scope and limitations

  • Myopic optimization. Agents react to current $I/N$, not anticipated future paths. Misses pre-emption behavior at the start of waves.
  • Rational expectations. Agents see true prevalence, not reported cases. Real populations over-react early and under-react late.
  • No endogenous vaccination choice. Vaccination is policy-controlled.
  • Single composite group in this page. The downloadable Python package implements the seven-layer bipartite structure.
  • Generic respiratory pathogen. No specific pathogen calibration; parameters are illustrative.
